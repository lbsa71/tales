# Example Draft — *The Spare Room*

Two voice-calibration excerpts. These are exploratory pages rather than locked script. Character names and specific business are provisional within the established architecture.

---

## Excerpt 1 — The bag

*From the first movement. The audience does not yet need to understand anything speculative.*

*RUTH sits at the table. HELEN stands behind her, holding her coat open. KIT has put a bag on an empty chair.*

**RUTH:** Is that for my things?

**KIT:** Yes.

**RUTH:** That's your swimming bag.

**KIT:** It's dry.

**RUTH:** I didn't ask whether it was wet.

**HELEN:** There's hardly anything to take.

**RUTH:** Good. It won't take long when I've decided.

**HELEN:** You have decided.

*RUTH holds out a hand. HELEN moves the coat towards it.*

**RUTH:** My glasses.

*HELEN gives her the glasses from the table.*

**KIT:** I can get another bag.

**HELEN:** The bag is fine.

**RUTH:** I'm sure it is.

**KIT:** That sounded—

**HELEN:** It's fine.

*KIT opens the bag.*

**KIT:** What first?

**HELEN:** The blue mug.

**RUTH:** No.

**HELEN:** You said you wanted your own things around you.

**RUTH:** Some of them.

**HELEN:** You use it every day.

**RUTH:** It's the one that's here.

*KIT picks up the mug.*

**RUTH:** There's tea in that.

*KIT puts it down.*

**KIT:** Right.

**HELEN:** I'll drink it.

**RUTH:** It's cold.

**HELEN:** I don't mind.

*HELEN has to put the coat down to pick up the mug. RUTH takes the coat and folds it across her knees.*

**KIT:** I could start with clothes.

**RUTH:** You could sit down.

**KIT:** I've only got—

**HELEN:** We know.

**RUTH:** I don't.

**KIT:** I said I'd be back in an hour.

**RUTH:** Then you'd better not sit down.

*HELEN drinks.*

**RUTH:** I told you.

**HELEN:** It's all right.

**RUTH:** Your face says otherwise.

**HELEN:** Mum.

**RUTH:** What?

*HELEN puts the mug down.*

**HELEN:** We said we would do this today.

**RUTH:** You said today.

**HELEN:** And you said that would be—

**RUTH:** I said today was better than Thursday.

**HELEN:** Yes.

**RUTH:** I have something on Thursday.

**HELEN:** You could have told me that was what you meant.

**RUTH:** It was in the same sentence.

**KIT:** What's on Thursday?

**HELEN:** It doesn't matter what's on Thursday.

**RUTH:** It does to me.

*A pause.*

**HELEN:** Kit. You were here.

**KIT:** For which bit?

**HELEN:** When we discussed it.

**KIT:** I came in at the end.

**HELEN:** You heard her say today would be better.

**KIT:** I heard Thursday.

**HELEN:** You must have heard more than the word Thursday.

**KIT:** I was looking for my bag.

*They all look at the bag.*

**RUTH:** There it is.

*KIT almost laughs. HELEN sees.*

**KIT:** Sorry.

**HELEN:** I asked you to come and help.

**KIT:** I am trying to help. What goes in the bag?

*RUTH starts unfolding the coat.*

*HELEN moves to assist her. RUTH folds it again, more neatly.*

**RUTH:** Don't put clothes in with the mug.

**KIT:** I haven't put anything in.

**RUTH:** I can see that.

*KIT lifts the bag off the chair and puts it on the floor. To sit down, they move the chair away from the table.*

**HELEN:** Leave that where it was.

**RUTH:** It's in the way there.

**HELEN:** It's always been there.

**RUTH:** Yes.

*KIT remains standing, holding the chair.*

**KIT:** Here?

**RUTH:** That's fine.

**HELEN:** There isn't room.

*KIT sits.*

**KIT:** There is.

*HELEN looks at the empty bag. She picks it up and holds it open herself.*

**HELEN:** Your scarf, then. Can we put your scarf in?

*RUTH considers this.*

**RUTH:** The light one.

*HELEN finds it beneath the coat. She places it in the bag.*

**RUTH:** If we go.

## Excerpt 2 — What was promised

*From the transition between movements five and six. The audience now understands where Ruth lives.*

*The bag contains a few things. The coat is back on its chair. KIT has been standing near the entrance but has not left.*

**KIT:** Are you going to expect me to do this for you?

**HELEN:** Of course not.

**RUTH:** I didn't expect it either.

*A pause.*

**KIT:** What happened, then?

**HELEN:** There wasn't a ceremony.

**RUTH:** You asked me.

**HELEN:** Yes.

**RUTH:** You were on the floor.

**HELEN:** Don't.

**KIT:** You don't have to tell me.

**RUTH:** I'd been trying to get her to sit on a chair. She wouldn't.

**HELEN:** I could sit on a chair.

**RUTH:** I know. You wouldn't.

**HELEN:** I remember.

**RUTH:** You asked me to stay until you were asleep.

**HELEN:** That was one night.

**RUTH:** At first.

*HELEN picks up a mug, sees there is nothing in it, and puts it down.*

**RUTH:** You wanted me to keep talking. I told you about the shopping.

**HELEN:** All right.

**RUTH:** I'd bought two kinds of potatoes. You wanted to know why.

**HELEN:** I was trying to keep you talking.

**RUTH:** I know that now. I gave you quite a long answer.

*A small silence.*

**KIT:** And then she stayed?

**RUTH:** For a while. I had somewhere to go.

**HELEN:** I know.

**RUTH:** I'd arranged it.

**HELEN:** I know you had.

**RUTH:** Then you asked me to stay here.

**HELEN:** Yes.

**RUTH:** “As long as you like.”

**HELEN:** I know what I said.

**RUTH:** Well.

**HELEN:** I know what I said, Mum. You don't have to keep saying it.

**KIT:** You told me she'd always been here.

**HELEN:** As far as you were concerned. As far as you remembered.

**KIT:** That isn't what I thought you meant.

**HELEN:** I wasn't giving you a history.

**RUTH:** No.

*HELEN turns towards her.*

**RUTH:** You weren't.

**HELEN:** I asked you because I needed you. I haven't denied that.

**RUTH:** You never come and see me.

**HELEN:** I was here yesterday.

**RUTH:** You answered me yesterday.

**HELEN:** I answered you several times.

**RUTH:** You were somewhere else.

**HELEN:** Kit was talking to me.

**RUTH:** I didn't hear Kit.

**HELEN:** No. You couldn't. I could hear both of you.

**RUTH:** Why didn't you say?

**HELEN:** I did.

**RUTH:** You said, “One minute.”

**KIT:** You said that to me.

**HELEN:** I said it to both of you.

*A pause.*

**RUTH:** I waited.

**KIT:** So did I.

**HELEN:** And this is what I—

**KIT:** You didn't come back.

**HELEN:** I know. I'm sorry.

**KIT:** You said you'd call.

**HELEN:** Yes.

**RUTH:** What was it?

**KIT:** It doesn't matter now.

**HELEN:** It does matter.

**KIT:** Then you could have called.

*HELEN starts to speak, then stops.*

**HELEN:** Yes.

*RUTH reaches for the flask. The lid resists her. HELEN takes it automatically and loosens it.*

**RUTH:** Thank you.

*HELEN puts the flask down beside her.*

**RUTH:** I wouldn't have called if I'd known.

**HELEN:** Sometimes I'm not doing anything.

**RUTH:** What?

**HELEN:** When you call. Sometimes there isn't someone else. I'm not busy.

**RUTH:** All right.

**HELEN:** I might just be sitting.

**RUTH:** You're allowed to sit.

**HELEN:** I know.

**RUTH:** You make it sound as though I stand over you.

**HELEN:** I haven't said that.

**RUTH:** You could tell me you wanted to sit by yourself.

**HELEN:** I have.

**RUTH:** Then sit by yourself.

*HELEN sits.*

*They remain there.*

*KIT looks towards the entrance.*

**RUTH:** Not now. I didn't mean now.

**HELEN:** When?

**RUTH:** We're in the middle of something.

*HELEN gets up again.*

**RUTH:** Where are you going?

**HELEN:** Nowhere.

*She stays standing.*

**RUTH:** I told them I wouldn't be needing the other place. When you asked me to stay.

**HELEN:** I know.

**RUTH:** I was pleased.

*HELEN looks at her.*

**RUTH:** You didn't have to talk me into it.

*A silence.*

**HELEN:** I wasn't trying to—

*She stops.*

**RUTH:** Have you had anything today?

**HELEN:** Please don't.

**RUTH:** I'm asking.

**HELEN:** Yes.

**RUTH:** What?

**HELEN:** I can't remember.

*RUTH pours from the flask into the mug nearest her. She pushes it towards HELEN.*

**HELEN:** I don't want another one.

*RUTH leaves it where it is.*

*KIT picks up the bag. Something inside has caught in the zip. They work at it.*

*HELEN sits. After a moment, she takes the mug.*

## What these pages are testing

- Three distinct forms of evasion
- Humour that comes from obstructed action
- A speculative rule shown through unequal availability
- Ruth's care remaining real within the conflict
- Helen's need remaining legitimate after the invitation is exposed
- Kit refusing to function as a neutral witness
- Props remaining ordinary while their histories gain pressure
- Silence occupied by stage business rather than commentary
