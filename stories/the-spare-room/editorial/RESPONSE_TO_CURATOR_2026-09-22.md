# Response to the curator and second-draft changes

22 September 2026. Prepared for the author; not sent externally.

The central criticism is accepted. Continuity repairs made the first draft internally consistent, but did not establish that a company could communicate its essential distinctions economically. The second draft therefore changes both the shape of the conversation and what performers must show. It is a completed textual rework awaiting a blocked workshop, not evidence of production readiness.

## What changed, and why

| Critique | Decision and change |
|---|---|
| Corrective exchanges repeatedly explain an understood point. | Accepted. Nine movements become seven. Opening consent disputes are compressed into one failed packing attempt; the later encounter rehearsal is folded into departure preparation. The travelling yeses, household arithmetic and final correction about promises are removed. |
| Everyone shares the same polished rhetorical skill. | Accepted. Helen gets absorbed and overexplains her work or an uncomfortable memory; Ruth wanders through particular stories and practical concerns; Kit rushes, overpromises and cannot repair an injury neatly. Precision remains available, especially to Ruth, but no longer determines every exchange. |
| There is too little life beyond the family thesis. | Accepted. Kit's burnt crust is anticipated before it is missed. The poorly made bed, harbour visitor, cherry cake and unfinished dog story give the people pleasures and interests that do not simply conclude the argument. The dance includes an ordinary wrong turn and laughter rather than a polished routine. |
| The audience may be unsure what play it is watching for too long. | Accepted in substance; timing qualified by the author's choice. The revelation remains gradual. Matched exits establish unequal access; a plain request, cold and the unspoken shoe establish sharing; near the end of the successful trial Helen explicitly names both Ruth's residence and the room within her. Essential clarity no longer rests on repeated ambiguous uses of "here" and "back." |
| Abstract directions leave the central invention to exceptional actors. | Accepted. A shared sightline, concrete reactions, ordinary conversation, explicit withdrawal and paired calls replace directions requiring an actor to embody vast consciousness. Helen is still her own contextual self-representation inside her mind. She does not have to demonstrate a new body. |
| The second encounter experiment repeats the argument. | Accepted. Ruth now asks to try Helen's experience. She receives it, finds it uncomfortable, asks to stop and is promptly released. No reassuring agreement results, but both have done something new. |
| The ending can become commentary on its own significance. | Accepted. Kit misses a previously anticipated pleasure, asks for company, begins to leave and is recalled by Helen. Helen then sets aside an established activity and takes the offered hand. No speech explains what this proves. Kit's private access remains closed. |
| Further confidence requires a blocked workshop. | Accepted. The workshop guide specifies actual props, unbriefed spectators, no read-aloud stage directions and separate collection of comprehension, attention and actor difficulties. No such evidence is claimed yet. |

## What has been preserved or qualified

The slow reveal was deliberately retained at the author's request. Greater clarity comes from cumulative evidence and one direct confirmation, not an opening lecture. The wider civilisation can remain partly unexplained; confusion about whether a departure ends residence cannot.

The script remains science fiction. It does not introduce a mechanism, a test of Ruth's authenticity or a doubt about whether she is alive. Her move leads to a life she wants to try, with ordinary uncertainty about whether she will enjoy it.

The quiet trial succeeds. Ruth's departure is therefore a choice about the life she wants, not a punishment for being unable to remain quiet. Helen still stops the move, Kit still uses the memory against Helen, and the women still fail to agree about future recognisability.

The bag, chair, sleeve and hand remain because they change what people do. They are not devices to be decoded independently of the scene. The bag cannot trap or transport Ruth.

Humour and verbal intelligence have not been banned. The revision removes recurring structures and verdicts, rather than making every line hesitant or every joke consequential. The new text still requires performers with ordinary craft; the aim is to reduce the number of essential facts dependent on minute interpretation.

No fixed "15%" improvement is claimed. Nor is a shorter word count proof of a more enjoyable evening. Approximately seventy minutes remains an unmeasured ambition. The workshop must reveal the actual duration without manufactured pauses, and may show that further writing as well as cutting is needed.
