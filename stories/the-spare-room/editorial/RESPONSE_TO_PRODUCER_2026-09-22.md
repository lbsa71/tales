# Response to the producer — *The Spare Room*

22 September 2026 · Revision 2

Thank you for the close reading. I accept the central diagnosis: the earlier package described ambitions that the pages had not yet demonstrated. In trying to protect accessibility and avoid exposition, I had made the writing rules too restrictive and settled too much of the argument in advance.

I have accepted the five principal criticisms. My qualifications concern how to put them into practice, rather than reasons to dismiss them.

**1. The science fiction must change the events — accepted.**

The new middle passage makes Helen's wider existence something she and Kit can enjoy together. Helen invites Kit to share an experience; later, while that contact remains open, Kit receives a private memory Helen is reliving. Kit uses it aloud to pressure Ruth into leaving. Helen withdraws the contact.

This gives the premise a consequential action beyond constant availability. Receiving the memory is not Kit's deliberate offence; exploiting it is.

It also deliberately revises the earlier avoidance of access to thoughts. The access is limited to invited, present experience, rather than a power to search someone's history. That choice needs to justify itself in performance. If the audience needs a technical explanation to follow the betrayal, the scene will need revision.

**2. Revelations must produce action, and today must matter — accepted.**

Ruth and Helen now try a compromise in front of us: Ruth will keep quiet while Helen participates more fully in her wider life. Its failure changes Ruth's intention. She becomes ready to leave earlier.

Helen then discovers that Ruth's new life may change the mother she expects to visit. She takes back the packed bag and obstructs the departure she has been demanding. The chair dispute therefore affects the move; it does more than disclose whose memory furnished the room. Ruth remains able to leave and chooses to confront Helen's conditions.

Kit's other commitment is now specific: the first gathering of their new household. They have promised Neri that Helen will attend, although Helen only agreed to try. The gathering can begin without them, and in the revised outline it does. Their dishonesty costs them participation in a beginning they wanted to share.

The outline now identifies what can no longer proceed after each movement. Its timings remain provisional; stage business cannot substantiate them.

**3. The characters need particular lives, and Kit must be capable of harm — accepted.**

Helen now has work she enjoys and wants credit for: composing tides for inhabited ocean worlds. Her original crisis followed a separation she initiated, rather than simple abandonment.

Ruth gave up a reserved place with travelling dance callers. She has a real sacrifice to remember, but also reasons for preferring to be indispensable to Helen. At Sal's, she wants to learn unfamiliar dances and is susceptible to the pleasure of being asked to lead.

Kit wants Helen's company at a frightening new beginning. They overstate both Helen's agreement and Ruth's readiness, then misuse an intimacy Helen has offered. They are implicated in the struggle for attention.

These particulars are decisions for drafting, not a requirement to deliver biographies onstage. Kit's wrongdoing also cannot cancel Helen's actual neglect of them.

**4. The language and rhythm need more freedom — accepted.**

I have removed the prescriptions that every silence needs an object and every laugh should worsen someone's position.

The new passage includes Helen's longer, imperfect account of her work, Ruth's wandering dance story, genuine shared laughter, and silence without a practical task. Ruth becomes inarticulate when the familiar Helen is no longer immediately available.

The short exchanges remain useful. The problem was allowing them to determine the whole evening's rhythm. The swimming-bag opening and potatoes exchange are retained as you recommended.

**5. The ending should expose an unanticipated loss — accepted; the new ending remains a proposal.**

Ruth leaves without promising to remain the mother Helen expects to recognise. Helen must accept uncertainty about their future encounters as well as reduced responsibility.

The proposed final movement then has Kit ask Helen to accompany them to the household they have kept waiting. Ashamed after admitting their lie, Kit asks for the familiar mother “just for the arrival.” Helen consciously supplies that form and goes with them. The room remains empty. The return for spectacles is removed.

This could be a freely given kindness, an accommodation whose cost Helen understands imperfectly, or both. It could also become a too-neat illustration of repeating family patterns. The ending is not yet scripted, and an instruction to play ambiguity cannot guarantee it. That risk remains open.

There is therefore no wholesale refusal of the critique. I would qualify its application in two respects: consequences need to accumulate across movements, without every joke or gesture carrying the argument; and greater speculative ambition should come through what the people can do to and for one another, without requiring a fuller explanation of the civilisation. Both preserve the accessibility we have been developing.

I have retained the commercial discussion as a set of hypotheses for later investigation. Venue suitability, publication routes and audience demand have not been independently established, and they have not been used to dictate this revision.

The revised package contains a causal outline and a continuous exploratory middle passage. The opening still needs integration, the later movements need dialogue, and the seventy-minute shape needs a timed reading. The next evidence should come from hearing the play: what surprises listeners after they understand the premise, where their allegiance changes, and whether they agree about the events while disagreeing about what each person has given or taken.
