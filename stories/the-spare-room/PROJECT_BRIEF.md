# Project Brief — *The Spare Room*

This document records the source commission in working form. It is not a replacement for the original RfP; it extracts the constraints that govern development.

---

## Commission

Develop an original high-concept science-fiction play or screenplay in which the imagined world is enormous and the production required to depict it is almost nothing.

The preferred form is now a stage play of approximately seventy minutes for three performers in one simple room.

---

## Desired audience experience

The audience initially sees a few people dealing with an ordinary human problem. Details gradually reveal that the situation differs radically from what was assumed. Earlier remarks acquire new literal and emotional meanings.

The pleasure should come from successive acts of reinterpretation:

> Wait — if that is true, then what they said earlier means…

The future is discovered through dialogue, behaviour, consequences, and altered context. It is not delivered through opening exposition or technical vocabulary.

---

## Human inquiry

The larger question is what remains recognisably human after bodies, lifespan, reproduction, memory, intelligence, society, and the distinction between biological and artificial life have changed beyond present recognition.

The drama should reach this inquiry through what people want from one another:

- to be loved;
- to remain necessary;
- to become unavailable without causing injury;
- to be remembered accurately;
- to control one's own home and future;
- to avoid inheriting an obligation;
- to have another person believe one's account of the past.

No character should lecture on these themes.

---

## Production constraints

- 2–5 performers; current concept uses 3
- one simple playing space
- ordinary furniture and props
- ordinary or near-ordinary clothing
- basic lighting and sound only
- no projections, CGI, prosthetics, machinery, elaborate scenic changes, or futuristic devices

Minimalism must be fundamental to the premise rather than a reduced version of a visually expensive story.

---

## Tone

The work may be funny, moving, unsettling, intimate, mysterious, or absurd. Humour should arise from familiar behaviour under extraordinary circumstances.

The desired balance is now explicit:

> A family play whose impossible premise makes the family problem more acute.

It should absorb viewers who would never choose to attend “a science-fiction play.”

---

## Initial development deliverable

The first agreed step consisted of:

1. a practical, scene-by-scene outline of the seventy minutes;
2. the wants and reversals within each movement;
3. the pacing of speculative revelations;
4. an early dialogue sample before the audience fully understands the premise;
5. a later sample after ordinary family remarks have acquired their literal second meanings.

The first [outline](drafts/outline-v1.md) and [dialogue samples](drafts/dialogue-v1.md) are preserved.
