# Progress Summary — *The Spare Room*

## Current phase

**Architecture and voice calibration complete. Full script not begun.**

The project has a stable dramatic premise, room grammar, nine-movement outline, character voice rules, and two dialogue samples. It is ready for a continuous drafting test.

---

## What is locked

- Working title: *The Spare Room*
- Stage play, approximately 70 minutes, no interval
- Three performers: Ruth, Helen, Kit
- One unchanged room with ordinary furniture and props
- Immediate objective: Helen wants Ruth to agree to move today
- Basic premise understood by the audience around minute 25
- Ruth is an independent person who lives within Helen
- Residency gives Ruth continuing access; visiting does not
- Helen originally begged Ruth to stay and said “as long as you like”
- Kit asks whether Helen expects the same arrangement
- Ruth did not initially demand or expect it
- Helen has helped preserve the room and its contested objects
- The move gives Ruth an independent future
- Ruth can agree without forgiving or reassuring Helen
- The door has consistent ordinary meaning throughout

## Next drafting gate

Draft movements 1–4 as one continuous sequence of approximately 25–30 minutes.

The sequence passes if a cold reader can answer all of the following without being taught the technology:

1. What does Helen want today?
2. Why does Ruth resist?
3. Why is Kit unable to remain neutral?
4. What freedom does Kit possess that Helen lacks?
5. By the end, where does Ruth live and what practical problem does that create?

The sequence should also create at least two retrospective recognitions without withholding information unnaturally.

---

## Read-aloud test

After movements 1–4 exist, read them with three voices and time them. Check:

- whether the first seven minutes contain enough forward pressure;
- whether the jokes belong to the people rather than the writer;
- whether the audience can sense a rule before it is named;
- whether “You live in me” clarifies instead of announcing a twist;
- whether Ruth remains someone the audience might willingly spend time with;
- whether Helen's need is felt before her justification fails;
- whether Kit has made a choice rather than merely observed the other two.

Do not lock movements 5–9 more tightly until this sequence proves the voices and room grammar.

## Risk register

| Risk | Current safeguard |
|------|-------------------|
| The premise invites copy/original debate | Ruth's personhood is treated as ordinary; ontology vocabulary excluded |
| The first act becomes static | Packing, leaving, tea, chair, and coat create continuous obstructed action |
| The revelation arrives as exposition | Unequal availability is demonstrated before it is named |
| Helen becomes simply correct | Original invitation and preservation of the room implicate her |
| Ruth becomes simply coercive | She surrendered alternatives, has informed preferences, and gains an independent future |
| Kit becomes a therapist | Kit refuses and chooses concrete obligations |
| The objects become puzzle clues | Only the chair carries major disputed-history pressure; other props stay ordinary |
| The final exit becomes magical | Agreement changes residency before the door is crossed |
| The ending rewards separation too neatly | The play ends before proving whether freedom repairs the relationship |
