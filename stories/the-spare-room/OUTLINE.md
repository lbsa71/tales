# Outline — *The Spare Room*

Approximately seventy minutes, played continuously. These are movements of pressure rather than scenes requiring blackouts. Pauses, interruptions, and the physical business of preparing to leave carry part of the duration.

---

## Immediate action

Helen has come to help Ruth move. She needs Ruth to agree that she is moving today.

The room contains a table, three mismatched chairs, a flask, mugs, spectacles, a coat, and a bag. One entrance is established through use. The room does not transform.

For the draft, three facts are fixed:

1. Ruth can address Helen when Helen's attention is elsewhere.
2. Ruth cannot do this to Kit.
3. An agreed move ends Ruth's standing access. The threshold itself has no magical power.

---

## Movement 1 — The bag

**Minutes:** 0–7

### Wants

- Helen wants to begin packing.
- Ruth wants to reopen whether packing should happen.
- Kit wants an identifiable job they can finish before another commitment.

### Action

Helen arrives assuming the difficult decision has already been made. Ruth treats their previous agreement as permission to continue discussing it. Neither account is entirely dishonest.

Helen holds Ruth's coat ready for too long. Kit opens the bag, but every proposed item produces another conversation. Ruth notices that Helen has brought the wrong bag and that Kit has somewhere else to be.

### Turn

Helen asks Kit to confirm that Ruth agreed to move. Kit cannot honestly confirm it. Helen's supposed helper becomes an unreliable witness.

### Audience knowledge

This family has a history of arranging things around one another. No speculative information is necessary. The modest objective—putting a few things into a bag—has already become difficult.

### Prop progression

- The coat is offered prematurely and ends up folded across Ruth's knees.
- The mug cannot be packed because it still contains cold tea.
- The chair is moved to make room, beginning an apparently trivial disagreement about where it belongs.

---

## Movement 2 — A minute outside

**Minutes:** 7–14

### Wants

- Helen wants Kit's support in private.
- Ruth wants to remain included in any discussion concerning her.
- Kit wants to avoid becoming an intermediary.

### Action

Kit briefly leaves to attend to their own arrangements. Ruth calls after them; there is no answer. Kit returns when ready.

Helen later attempts to step away from the argument. Ruth continues addressing her. Helen answers from beyond the entrance, first by reflex and then with irritation. She returns before obtaining the solitude she wanted.

The contrast should register as recognisable family behaviour before it registers as impossible.

### Turn

Ruth asks what she is supposed to do when she needs something. Helen replies too sharply that Ruth does not always need something.

The discussion shifts from accommodation to whether Ruth's company itself has become an imposition.

### Audience knowledge

Helen's difficulty withdrawing is unusually literal. Kit possesses a freedom she does not.

---

## Movement 3 — The practical reasons

**Minutes:** 14–22

### Wants

- Helen wants the move to appear necessary.
- Ruth wants to establish that staying is perfectly possible.
- Kit wants a compromise that will let everyone finish.

### Action

Helen presents the advantages of the new place: company, independence, and people available when she is occupied.

Ruth has visited it. She asks specific questions and corrects several things Helen presents too favourably. Her objections are informed, sometimes petty, and occasionally decisive.

Helen falls back on how much she has to manage. Ruth points out that maintaining the present arrangement requires very little. Kit, trying to be fair, confirms this.

### Turn

Helen admits that she could continue. “I cannot keep doing this” has been allowed to stand in for “I do not want to keep doing this.”

Ruth offers to make herself less troublesome. Helen finds the offer harder to answer than an accusation.

### Audience knowledge

The practical burden does not explain Helen's need. References to her life elsewhere begin to exceed an ordinary domestic geography, but nobody pauses to explain them.

---

## Movement 4 — Where Ruth lives

**Minutes:** 22–29

### Wants

- Ruth wants new household rules that will let her stay.
- Helen wants the difference between quiet and solitude acknowledged.
- Kit wants to understand why the apparent compromises fail.

### Action

Ruth proposes fewer interruptions. Helen says they have tried this. They quarrel over a recent incident: Helen was speaking with Kit elsewhere, Ruth called, and Helen could hear both people.

During the quarrel, Helen says plainly that Ruth lives inside her. It is an impatient statement of something everyone present already knows.

By the end of this movement, the audience should understand that Ruth has a life within Helen. Helen can turn her attention elsewhere, but Ruth remains able to reach her. This familiar room is where they meet in a form both recognise.

### Turn

Kit realises this arrangement may concern their own future.

### Audience knowledge

Earlier entrances, interruptions, and complaints become literal without ceasing to be ordinary. Ruth's personhood is never questioned.

---

## Movement 5 — What children do

**Minutes:** 29–36

### Wants

- Kit wants to know what Helen expects of them.
- Helen wants to reassure Kit and retain their support.
- Ruth wants Helen to acknowledge how the arrangement began.

### Action

Kit asks whether Helen will expect the same provision from them.

Helen's denial is immediate and sincere. Ruth replies that she did not expect it either.

Helen tries to contain the history. Ruth insists that Helen asked her to stay. Kit had understood the situation as an obligation Helen inherited; they now discover it began as an invitation.

### Turn

Ruth says Helen begged her.

The line should land during ordinary business rather than as a prepared revelation.

### Audience knowledge

Helen helped create the condition from which she now wants release. Ruth has a claim founded on a specific promise.

---

## Movement 6 — The invitation

**Minutes:** 36–44

### Wants

- Ruth wants the original promise acknowledged accurately.
- Helen wants its circumstances taken into account.
- Kit wants both women to stop using them as a witness.

### Action

They reconstruct the beginning through disputed details: where Helen was sitting, whether Ruth had already packed, what Helen actually said.

Helen had been desperately lonely. Ruth stayed through an immediate crisis and then accepted an invitation to remain. She gave up another arrangement to do so.

Helen eventually confirms the words: “As long as you like.”

Ruth complains that Helen never visits. Helen says she is here constantly. Ruth distinguishes answering a call from coming to spend time with her.

Kit names a recent consequence of Helen's divided attention: something they tried to tell her was lost between interruptions. Helen tries to use this as evidence against Ruth. Kit refuses that role; Helen also failed to return to the conversation.

### Turn

Helen loses the possibility of making one person responsible for the whole problem. She still wants the move.

### Audience knowledge

The arrangement has provided real comfort and caused real neglect. Helen's larger life contains people and obligations Ruth scarcely knows.

---

## Movement 7 — The chair

**Minutes:** 44–53

### Wants

- Ruth wants a say in what constitutes home.
- Helen wants her work preserving it recognised.
- Kit wants to finish the practical task and leave.

### Action

Kit moves a chair to make space for the bag. Helen automatically puts it back. Ruth tells Kit to move it again.

The disproportionate argument reveals that the chair remains because Helen has retained this version of the old room. Ruth disliked the original and replaced it long ago. Helen has continued furnishing their shared space from a memory Ruth does not share.

The audience's understanding of all the objects changes. They are not simply old things that survived. Their continued presence expresses Helen's selections.

Ruth reveals that she has revisited the proposed new home independently and liked someone there. She allowed Helen to believe the place was wholly unsuitable.

### Turn

Ruth begins considering what she might choose for herself. Helen immediately starts advising and restricting those choices.

Kit notices. Ruth certainly notices.

### Audience knowledge

Helen also has an investment in keeping Ruth familiar. Moving will require Helen to relinquish authority as well as responsibility.

---

## Movement 8 — Terms

**Minutes:** 53–63

### Wants

- Helen wants an agreement and reassurance that the relationship will survive it.
- Ruth wants control over the terms of her departure.
- Kit wants a finite commitment of their own choosing.

### Action

Ruth asks practical questions with emotional force. Who chooses when they next meet? What happens if Helen does not answer? Does Helen intend to keep checking on her?

Helen offers more supervision than Ruth wants. She casually allocates a future task to Kit without asking.

Kit refuses the assigned task, then offers a smaller, specific act of help. Their refusal matters even though they remain involved.

Helen finally asks Ruth to move. She does not present the move as wholly for Ruth's benefit and does not ask Ruth to certify her as a good daughter.

### Turn

Ruth agrees while remaining hurt. She specifies a condition Helen dislikes: a first visit on Ruth's terms, a choice of company Helen cannot vet, or another part of the new life Helen will not oversee. Helen accepts it.

### Audience knowledge

The consequences of moving are clear before the departure. Ruth will live elsewhere. They can visit. Neither can guarantee how often the other will want to.

---

## Movement 9 — Leaving

**Minutes:** 63–70

### Wants

- Ruth wants to leave without being hurried, pitied, or managed.
- Helen wants to help without converting help into control.
- Kit wants to escort Ruth and then keep their own commitment.

### Action

The bag is finally packed. Some things Helen expected Ruth to take remain behind.

The coat catches at a cuff. Helen helps only after Ruth asks. Their familiarity remains useful and tender.

Ruth leaves with Kit. No embrace is compulsory.

A brief return for Ruth's spectacles should be tested in rehearsal. Ruth appears at the entrance and waits to be admitted. The action demonstrates her new status: she can return as a visitor. She is alive elsewhere and capable of forgetting something.

After the second departure, Helen begins to address her mother about a small matter. She stops.

### Final image under consideration

Helen may:

- move the disputed chair;
- sit in it;
- leave it exactly where Ruth placed it.

The choice should be made after hearing the full play aloud. The architecture commits to the departure but does not predetermine what the quiet means.

## Revelation and pressure map

| Minute | Family conflict | Expanded circumstance | Resulting pressure |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------------|--------------------|
| 0–7 | Did Ruth actually agree? | None required | Kit will not validate Helen's account |
| 7–14 | Who is allowed to withdraw? | Helen remains reachable | Helen's grievance becomes visible |
| 14–22 | Is the move necessary? | Staying costs almost nothing | Helen must admit desire rather than necessity |
| 22–29 | Why do compromises fail? | Ruth lives within Helen | Kit sees personal implications |
| 29–36 | Is this a child's duty? | Helen invited Ruth | Moral balance reverses |
| 36–44 | What was promised? | “As long as you like” | Love and obligation become inseparable |
| 44–53 | Whose home is this? | Helen maintains the room | Helen's attachment and control become visible |
| 53–63 | What will moving mean? | Independent residence and voluntary visits | Ruth gains terms; Kit limits inheritance |
| 63–70 | Can they actually separate? | Ruth can return as a visitor | Helen encounters the quiet she requested |

## Drafting flex

The outline fixes the causal spine, the timing of the principal revelations, and the room's rules. It deliberately leaves open:

- the exact crisis behind Helen's invitation;
- the precise nature of Ruth's opportunity elsewhere;
- which person or activity attracts Ruth to the new home;
- the smaller injuries and jokes through which old scores emerge;
- whether Ruth returns for her spectacles;
- Helen's final physical action.

Those should be discovered in dialogue and rehearsal rather than solved in advance.
