# Conceptual Map — The Spare Room

Revision 2, responding to the [producer review](editorial/PRODUCER_REVIEW_2026-09-22.md). These are working dramatic decisions, not proof that a seventy-minute play succeeds.

## The problem onstage

Helen has promised Kit she will try to be present at the beginning of Kit's new shared household this evening. Kit has told Neri that Helen definitely agreed. Helen intends to settle Ruth into an independent home first. Ruth has visited that home without Helen and knows more about it than either visitor assumes.

The arrangement could continue. Today matters because all three have made different promises about what happens after this visit. Someone else will be let down if those promises are treated as indefinitely revisable.

Ruth lives within Helen. Their room keeps available a mutually recognisable encounter: Ruth as mother, Helen in the bodily and conversational form she used at twenty-seven. The form is a present act by a living person. It is not a recording, duplicate, younger mind, or replay.

Outside this encounter Helen has lived for centuries and can participate in several places and shared perceptions at once. She enjoys that life. Sustaining the small, familiar encounter now costs her more than interruptions.

The question has widened: when someone loves the person you have been, what are they entitled to ask you to keep being?

## What the science fiction must do

The central attempted compromise is performed.

Ruth agrees to keep quiet. Helen tries remaining with her while also participating fully in Kit's company. Helen's body does not change, but her organisation of attention does. Kit recognises and enjoys this Helen. Ruth receives answers, yet cannot find the familiar daughter in them.

Helen invites Kit into a shared experience. Sharing attention gives access to what the other person is presently experiencing or remembering; it does not expose a searchable life history. When Ruth raises the old invitation, Helen remembers it privately. Kit, still included in her attention, acquires words Helen has never told them. Kit repeats those words in order to win the argument.

This is a new abuse of intimacy, accomplished in front of us. It is impossible to reduce to competing phone calls. The same capacity that enables a mother to live within a daughter enables a visitor to exceed an invitation.

There is no general mind-reading power. Entry requires Helen's invitation; Kit's wrongdoing is exploiting an intimacy already granted. The breach is addressed in conduct: Helen immediately withdraws the shared contact. Kit has to speak across the room again.

## Writer's private scale

The original invitation occurred 312 years before the action. Helen had just ended an eighty-year shared marriage with four other people. She wanted her own life and credit for work that the group treated as theirs. She initiated the separation and then discovered how frightening unshared experience could be.

Ruth kept talking during Helen's first night alone. The potatoes remain. A night's help became an invitation to remain as long as Ruth liked. Ruth gave up a place with a travelling group of dance callers to accept it.

The invitation has survived Helen's changing bodies, livelihoods, relationships, and communities. Ruth has continued living throughout; she has not waited inertly or repeated a stored day.

Helen now helps compose tides for inhabited ocean worlds. Her particular pleasure is feeling a harbour from both shores and from within the water. She enjoys praise and still resents shared credit.

Kit and Neri are joining seven other adults in a household that sometimes shares perception. This evening is their first common meal and shared experience. The others can proceed without Kit. Nothing explodes, expires, or erases anyone if Kit is late. An unrepeatable beginning occurs without them, and Neri learns Kit falsely represented Helen's agreement.

These facts give the writer continuity. The script needs only the facts that someone uses, gives away, or refuses.

## Rules that remain clear

- Three autonomous people are present. Different modes of attention do not create additional characters.
- The room belongs within Helen's life and remains when it is empty.
- An entrance joins the current encounter. An exit leaves it. Neither changes a person's existence.
- Ruth's residence gives her standing access to address Helen, not automatic knowledge of Helen's other conversations.
- Helen usually responds by supplying the familiar meeting form. She can resist or attempt another form; doing so changes the encounter and may hurt Ruth.
- Invited shared attention extends the same capacity for co-presence. It does not authorise Kit to use everything encountered.
- Moving ends Ruth's standing access by agreement. The doorway carries out a departure already chosen.
- Ruth can leave unaided. Kit is an offered escort, not a necessary transport mechanism.
- Remembered objects belong to Helen's memory. Ruth can refuse to inhabit that remembered arrangement in future; she cannot order Helen to forget her childhood.

The audience should follow those consequences. They do not need a mechanism for producing them.

## The chair must cause an obstruction

Ruth becomes ready to leave around the midpoint. She wants to join Sal's shared house and try leading dances without keeping her old body or the familiar mother-role.

Helen's plan has assumed something narrower: independent accommodation with the same Ruth inside it. She offers to preserve the room as their regular meeting place and begins prescribing the first visit.

Ruth moves the chair out of its habitual position and refuses that proposed arrangement. Helen restores it and takes the packed bag back from Kit. She says they are not going anywhere on this understanding.

The daughter demanding separation now prevents a departure. The chair has forced a choice, not merely disclosed a theme.

## Three compromised wants

Helen wants privacy, the pleasure of her wider existence, and reliable access to the familiar mother who helped her survive becoming separate.

Ruth wants release from Helen's remembered version of her and also wants the daughter she recognises to remain available on demand. Her own appetite for change does not make her generous about Helen's.

Kit wants Helen at the new household's beginning and fears going without a recognisable parent. They have sold Helen's attendance to Neri and Ruth's readiness to Helen. They can see coercion clearly when it is directed at them.

## Ending under trial

The move happens. Ruth declines an escort and refuses to promise to remain the familiar mother for future visits. Helen keeps her memories and room, but loses the right to treat their preservation as Ruth's agreement to appear in them.

Kit has missed the first shared experience with Neri and the others. They can still go; the missed beginning cannot be supplied retrospectively. They ask Helen to accompany them in her familiar form, just for the arrival.

Helen deliberately supplies that form and leaves with Kit.

Play the act without a verdict. It could be a chosen gift, a concession to shame, favouritism, or the opening of another indefinite promise. It must not be staged as possession or automatic regression. The audience can agree about the choice and disagree about what it costs.

The spectacles return is removed from this draft. It would add reassurance after the new problem has arrived.

## What remains uncertain

The performance distinction between Helen's modes may fail or resemble ordinary code-switching. The shared-attention breach must therefore be legible as an acquired experience, not a lucky guess or familiar anecdote.

The expanded middle may be too eventful or over-explained. Rehearsal must decide how much of the private history survives into spoken dialogue.

The ending may read as a simple warning that families repeat themselves. If so, revise the action or preceding evidence; an instruction to perform ambiguity cannot create it.

Ruth's and Helen's rights are not resolved by a final maxim. The play must find what these particular people can bear doing.
