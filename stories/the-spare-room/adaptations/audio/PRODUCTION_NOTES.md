## Cast and narration

Use **four performers** for the first test: Ruth, Helen, Kit and a separate narrator. Three distinct character voices matter more than strong age effects or accents. Ruth is a capable living person, not a frailty performance; Kit is an adult. Helen needs room to enjoy her work as well as manage the family. Sal, Neri, the harbour child and remembered people remain unvoiced.

The narrator reports physical actions in a plain, close voice. Let the action happen with the words; do not add a second long pause to repeat what has just been narrated. The 21 short narration cues name objects and participants where sound alone would be ambiguous. They supply neither motives nor a lecture on the world.

If only three performers are available, propose the Kit performer doubling narration in a separately recorded, neutral register, with a stable change of vocal delivery and microphone treatment that survives mono. Do not ask them to improvise live switches in the bag, sleeve or hand passages. Test whether listeners hear an independent narrative function, especially when that voice describes Kit. If they attribute the narration to Kit, use a fourth performer; a pitch effect is not an adequate repair. Record which arrangement was actually tested.

## Recording setup

General reading, room and harbour conventions are in [CONVENTIONS.md](CONVENTIONS.md). The script retains cues at the point of action.

Use three chairs, a table, mugs and flask, glasses, coat, scarf and ordinary swimming bag for the performed actions. A single quiet recording space and one water recording are sufficient. Object sounds can be captured with the actors or added as simple pickups. The narrated handle, sleeve and hand actions carry their own information; elaborate foley is unnecessary. Keep an ordinary low room tone through pauses. Do not fill it with a clock, score or meaningful electronic hum.

For the first development listening export, use no spoken credits; identify title, cast and recording conditions in the file metadata and workshop record. Any later spoken credits must be scripted, counted and timed.

## Editorial choices in draft 1.0

The source order remains useful: failed agreement, successful trial, chosen move, Helen's obstruction, Kit's breach, the private duet, departure and company. Audio changes concentrate that sequence rather than introducing a new frame.

- **A1, opening:** shortened agreement repetitions and the bed recollection; retained cold tea, ruined scarf, the kept terrible bed and Kit's new sleeping arrangements. The burnt-toast story and “my people” anticipation remain so Kit has an attractive life to miss. Paired unanswered/answered calls establish the unusual dependence before anyone names it.
- **A2, harbour:** removed the blue-shoes observer and shortened geography and the Friday-hall setup. Retained the child spoiling Helen's beautiful arrangement, her pleasure in the open water, the credit exchange and a compact dance demonstration. Its narrated wrong turn lets the family share physical humour in audio. The quiet trial finishes successfully before Ruth asks for different company and chooses Sal.
- **A3, packing:** kept the cake and crumbs almost intact. Sal's welcome is a reason to want the move. Reduced repeated visit planning; the chair's displacement now leads directly to Helen taking the bag. Narration makes the two handles and Ruth's release unmistakable without explaining them.
- **A4, breach:** retained the damaging use of the fragment and Kit's failed repair. Helen's formerly silent request to stop Ruth's recollection is now “Mum. Please.” Kit's interrupted account of Neri gives their grievance room without justifying the breach. Access stays closed.
- **A5, duet:** retained the old fear, the missed callers' opportunity, Ruth's imperfect account of staying and Helen's wish to be named for her work. Lightly trimmed the dog-story setup; its failed recollection and shared laughter still precede Helen putting down the bag. Ruth's separate attempt to share the harbour remains a failed connection, not a reason to forbid her move.
- **A6–A7, leaving:** shortened the bag-gift setup, preserving its ordinary history. Narration gives the sleeve refusal and final offered, nearly withdrawn and accepted hand their full sequence. Added Kit's explicit “I can't see it” at the harbour's return. Helen recalls Kit after they reach the entrance, takes their hand and begins the walk.

Text review during preparation restored Kit's household anticipation, clarified prop handling and removed a water onset at the moment of exclusion that could have muddied the listener's perspective. These are editorial checks, not performance or audience evidence. No staged or recorded test has informed draft 1.0.

A provisional 3–6 minutes of additional, non-overlapping silence, laughter, handling and sound would give a broad **36–45-minute planning range**. No one has performed or measured that allowance. Forty-five minutes is near the long end of this range, so a natural read may finish appreciably short. Do not assign silence lengths or slow actors to reach it. Time the whole piece first. If a shorter read earns its turns, recommend retaining the measured duration with a stated deviation. If listeners feel hurried or uninvolved, develop the identified relationship or action; do not add generic exposition. If long, examine repeated negotiations before cutting laughter or the time needed to refuse, recover or choose.

## Questions for the first session

- Do listeners enjoy the family before they have solved the room? Does A1 earn the time before the harbour?
- Does the retained dance demonstration give pleasure, or interrupt Helen's activity without enough return?
- Does the completed quiet trial register as a success, and Sal's welcome as an attraction?
- Is the bag's obstruction plain while remaining an ordinary possession? Is the remembered chair consequential?
- Is Kit's accidental receipt distinguishable from their deliberate misuse? Does H3 continuing after Ruth's request confuse anyone about whose experience persists?
- Does the dog story permit connection, or lose attention after its point is understood?
- Does the sleeve refusal hurt without narration naming the hurt? Is Helen's final choice audible, including the option to let Kit go and the fact their access remains closed?
