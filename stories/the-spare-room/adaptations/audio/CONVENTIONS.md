# The Spare Room — reading and performance conventions

Companion to [SCRIPT.md](SCRIPT.md), audio draft 1.0. This document is not spoken. Roles and source details are in [PREAMBLE.md](PREAMBLE.md); casting, recording and editorial notes are in [PRODUCTION_NOTES.md](PRODUCTION_NOTES.md).

## Reading the script

Only paragraphs beginning **RUTH:**, **HELEN:**, **KIT:** or **NARRATOR:** are spoken. Bracketed paragraphs are production or performance cues. Titles and section headings are not read. Play continuously, without section announcements or transitions. Sal, Neri and all other people mentioned have no recorded voices.

`PLAY`, `SOUND` and `VOICE` identify non-spoken cues at the point of action. Section IDs A1–A7 are recording and feedback references; they do not create breaks in the listening experience.

## The room

The room has an ordinary, close acoustic which never changes into an exterior. Quiet object sounds may support actions; they need not illustrate every movement. An exit is a few ordinary steps, without a door effect or special threshold sound. Calls are conversational. Helen's replies from outside remain clear and untreated. No music.

## The harbour and shared experience

H1–H4 are selective glimpses of HELEN'S present harbour experience, audible to the audience. They are not sounds in the room and do not announce who shares her experience. Use the same small wash of water against stone, centred and quiet, with no voices or digital effects. Room acoustics and dialogue remain unchanged. Each cue in the script names the invited participant, if any. Fading a glimpse changes the listening focus, not anyone's access.

The listener stays with the room while sometimes hearing a little of Helen's wider experience. All character speech is public speech in that room, apart from her explicitly cued replies from outside. No whispered thought track, memory reenactment or technological sound is needed.

| Cue | Listening perspective and current invitation | Audible treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1, A2 | Helen's present harbour; Kit has just been invited; Ruth is not sharing. | Small wash against stone, below unchanged room voices. Kit feels cold and checks dry feet; Ruth says she cannot hear it. Recede during conversation without implying closure. |
| H2, A2 | Helen's approaching wave, still shared with Kit alone. | Approach, shared reaction and settling. Ruth completes the quiet trial without interruption. No crowd voices. |
| No harbour cue at withdrawal, A4 | Kit loses the invited experience. Helen keeps it; Ruth's residence is unaffected. | Spoken withdrawal and Kit's response carry the change. No switch, silence effect or new water onset. |
| H3, A5 | Helen's experience, separately offered to Ruth; Kit remains excluded. | Same water. Ruth's share ends immediately when she asks; the sound continues briefly through Helen's reply before receding, because the audience's perspective is Helen's. |
| H4, A7 | Helen alone; Kit explicitly cannot see it. | Quiet water under the last exchange, receding when Helen chooses to set the activity aside and take Kit's hand. No reopening of access. |

These are selective auditory glimpses, not an access indicator. Silence between them does not mean Helen has lost the harbour. A small, recognisable sound and explicit dialogue should establish its ownership; this remains a listening-test question. Keep all decisive information centred and intelligible on one ordinary speaker. Spatial panning may add atmosphere but must carry no rule or action.

Helen's harbour is not a phone, screen or exterior setting. Keep her room voice and ability to answer Ruth intact. Do not drown a reply to show divided attention. The unspoken shoe prepares Kit's later receipt of an unspoken fragment; no sound dramatises the remembered night. Kit receives Helen's present recollection through the invitation that remained open, then chooses to use it against her.

Treat all exits alike acoustically: ordinary steps, without a special door or transition effect. The dialogue distinguishes leaving an encounter from moving residence. Ruth carries a scarf in the bag and glasses in her coat; the bag never carries Ruth. Her departure has no final breath, dissolve, spiritual music or extinction of the room.
