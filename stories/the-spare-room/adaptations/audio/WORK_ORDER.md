# Work order — The Spare Room: 45-minute audio adaptation

Issued 23 September 2026.

## Commission

Adapt the English stage second draft into an approximately **45-minute audio drama with separate character voices and sparse narration**. Deliver a complete script and practical materials for a modest test recording, then use a performed listening test to assess and revise the work when performers are available.

The aim is a more concentrated emotional experience: listeners should enjoy the family, feel its injuries and want to hear what happens next. Understanding the speculative premise is necessary but does not, by itself, make the adaptation successful.

Treat the piece as an audio work in its own right. Change dialogue, compress or combine passages, and alter the running order where that improves the experience. Preserve the decisive relationships and events rather than every stage line or movement division.

This order covers adaptation and a development recording/test. It does not commission a finished sound master, publication, a stage rewrite or a translation. No budget or delivery deadline is specified here. Do not report a planned recording or test as completed evidence.

## Form and duration

Use Ruth, Helen and Kit as the three character roles, plus a distinct narration function. Do not add speaking roles for Sal or Neri. Propose whether narration needs a separate performer or can be doubled without confusing the listener; three characters do not automatically mean only three performers.

Aim for 45 minutes including narration, sound, intentional silences and any credits. This is an artistic target, not a reason to pad or accelerate a performance. Time a natural performed read first and report any deviation with an editorial recommendation.

The source contains approximately 4,650 spoken dialogue words. At illustrative delivery rates of 120–140 words per minute, that represents about 33–39 minutes before added narration and pauses. This is planning arithmetic, not a verified runtime or an instruction to actors. Reading all the stage directions would add approximately 2,410 words and is not the intended form.

## Editorial priorities

Concentrate emotional movement rather than simply shortening every pause. Allow embarrassment, pleasure, refusal and loss to register. Fewer conversational passages, fully played, are preferable to the existing text rushed through.

Examine the opening and harbour sequence first; together they account for about 44 percent of the source dialogue. Review the length of the bed recollection, subsidiary harbour incidents, repeated visit arrangements, the dog story and departure preliminaries. None is subject to an automatic cut: retain what earns its time through pleasure, character or a consequential change.

Protect the functions of these passages:

- Ruth's cake and welcome at Sal's make the new life desirable.
- Kit's anticipation of the burnt-crust ritual makes missing it a loss.
- Helen's original fear explains the dependence without excusing everything it became.
- Shared humour gives the family something worth losing.
- Ruth's own attempt to share Helen's experience creates a new risk and disappointment.

Keep the characters capable of pettiness, injury and incomplete understanding. Do not turn the adaptation into an orderly demonstration of healthy boundaries. Equally, do not restore the first draft's continuous cycle of statement, correction and polished verdict.

## Events and relationships to preserve

The move initially stalls because agreement has been overstated. The quiet trial succeeds in practical terms; Ruth then chooses to try a life she wants elsewhere. Her departure must not become punishment for failing the trial.

Helen becomes the person obstructing the move when she realises Ruth may change beyond familiar recognition. The remembered chair and withheld bag must affect the action. The bag is an ordinary possession, not a container or transport mechanism for Ruth.

Kit receives a fragment of Helen's present recollection through an invitation that remains open. Kit's offence is exploiting that fragment, not deliberately searching Helen's past. Helen withdraws access; it stays closed through the ending. Ruth's later invitation is separate and ends promptly when she asks.

Ruth is alive and autonomous. Moving to Sal's ends her automatic ability to summon Helen, while leaving future visits possible. Ordinary exits earlier in the play do not end her residence. Do not imply death, deletion or a test of her authenticity.

The sleeve refusal must remain an injury: Ruth refuses Helen's help and accepts Kit's. At the ending Kit can leave, Helen recalls them, and Helen freely chooses company and takes the initiative in their departure. This does not restore Kit's private access or settle every hurt.

## Narration and sound

Write narration for listening, not as a reading of rehearsal instructions. Use it economically to make consequential physical actions available: the women holding the bag, the sleeve refusal and the final offered, nearly withdrawn and accepted hand. Preserve the significance of the chair through action and context.

Report observable actions without explaining their emotional meaning. For example, “Ruth takes one handle. Helen keeps the other. Neither pulls” can preserve an image. An explanation that Helen cannot bear to let her mother go would remove the listener's discovery. Avoid unnecessary narration of every glance, pause or chair movement.

Retain a gradual discovery of the unusual world, while ensuring listeners understand the decisive actions when they occur. Do not begin with a lecture about the room, minds or technology. The wider civilisation may remain mysterious; the consequences of invitation, withdrawal and moving must be followable.

Specify whose experience each subjective sound represents. Harbour sound must not accidentally imply physical relocation, that everyone hears what Helen hears, or that Helen loses the harbour when Kit's access ends. A listener must be able to follow the piece on an ordinary speaker as well as headphones; left/right positioning alone must not carry an essential fact.

Use restrained sound where it adds information or experience. Do not add a technological mechanism through digital effects, make departure sound like death or transcendence, or rely on music to supply an emotion the scene has not earned. Helen's wider existence should retain its strangeness rather than becoming merely a distracting screen or phone call.

## Listening test and acceptance

Use listeners unfamiliar with the script, including some who do not normally seek science fiction. Give them no synopsis, visible manuscript or explanation of the mind-room. Gather individual reactions before group discussion.

Test enjoyment and emotional involvement alongside clarity:

- Where did you want to keep listening, and where did your attention wander?
- Which moment moved, amused or unsettled you? Which moment remained afterwards?
- Whose company did you enjoy? Would you recommend the piece to someone, and why?
- Where does Ruth live, and when did you become reasonably certain?
- Why did Helen stop the move? What did Kit receive, and what did they do with it?
- What happened with the bag and sleeve? What changes when Ruth leaves?
- What happened at the final handholding? What could Helen have chosen instead?

The required standard is agreement about basic events and available choices, with room for disagreement about motives, generosity and future consequences. Listeners need not reconstruct the civilisation or agree on a moral lesson.

Log attention losses and misunderstandings against identifiable passages. Distinguish confusing action, repetitive action and insufficient emotional investment; they require different revisions. Do not treat correct comprehension as proof of an enjoyable work, or silence as automatic proof of boredom.

After the test, revise the script against the findings and record what changed and what remains unresolved. Confirm the revised timing through a further performed read where material changes affect duration. Acceptance as a tested development draft requires the complete script, usable production conventions, actual performance timing and a candid listening report. A finished manuscript alone may be delivered as a writing milestone but must remain labelled untested.
