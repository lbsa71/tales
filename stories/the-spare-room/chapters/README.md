## Cast and space

- **Ruth:** Helen's mother.
- **Helen:** Ruth's daughter and Kit's mother.
- **Kit:** Helen's adult child.

Neri and Sal are neither heard nor seen. One ordinary room, one entrance, a table, three movable chairs, a flask with tea, three mugs, spectacles, a coat, a scarf and a swimming bag. Ordinary clothes. No effects, recorded speech, projections or scenic changes.

Helen is represented inside her own mind as her own idea of herself in these family encounters. The room is also within her. All three people are alive and autonomous. These facts are for rehearsal; do not give spectators a preliminary explanation.

## Playable conventions

All printed dialogue is public. Ordinary speech in the room, invited shared experience and Ruth's residence are different relationships.

For the harbour, Helen and the invited person look towards the same empty area and react to specified events there. Nothing physically appears. The view has no permanent stage location or portal; the shared sightline simply lets spectators follow their activity. Helen continues to respond to Ruth. No change of body, trance or complex movement vocabulary is required.

Kit receives an unspoken detail and later a fragment of Helen's present recollection. They do not search her memories. Helen explicitly closes the view; Kit remains able to converse. Ruth can still call Helen outside the room until Ruth moves out. Use the same entrance and ordinary volume for each paired calling demonstration.

Ruth's brief invited view in movement five ends when she asks. Kit's view remains closed throughout the rest of the play. At the ending Helen gives Kit her company and hand, not renewed access to her private experience.

The bag does not contain or transport Ruth. Her final departure carries out an agreed move; it requires no prop ritual. The room remains.
