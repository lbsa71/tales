# The Spare Room writer brief

22 September 2026 · Handoff revision 3

We would like you to develop *The Spare Room* into a continuous stage play of approximately seventy minutes. The concept has had enough discussion to support drafting. This package gives you the present structure, exploratory dialogue and the decisions behind them. The next creative deliverable, once commission terms are agreed, should be a complete first draft.

## The play

Helen wants her mother, Ruth, to move out of the home she has made for her inside herself. Kit, Helen's adult child, has come to help, partly because they want Helen's attention for a new beginning of their own.

The audience should recognise the family before understanding where they are. As its understanding expands, the impossible circumstances should change what the people can do to one another. The room and its objects remain materially simple while their meaning changes.

The emotional starting point is: can you love your mother and still want her out of your head? A further problem emerges when Helen discovers that an independent Ruth may no longer be the familiar mother she expects to visit.

## What to protect

- Three performers, one stable playing space, ordinary furniture, objects and clothes. No additional voices or effects-dependent events.
- An emotionally accessible family argument, with pleasure and humour as well as hurt.
- Ruth as an autonomous, living person. Her move leads to another life.
- Consistent entrances and exits. The door marks participation in the encounter; an agreement changes residence.
- Helen's sincere original invitation. The present conflict renegotiates something that once helped both women.
- Freedom for the characters to be partial, petty, mistaken and surprising.

## The two practical clarifications

**Sharing a view is different from living here.** Kit's invitation permits a temporary sharing of Helen's present sensory attention. Closing it removes that view. Kit can still speak normally in the room. Ruth's residence lets her address Helen even after Helen leaves the encounter; Helen can refuse to answer but cannot stop receiving the call while leaving that residence intact. In the revised passage, Kit loses the harbour, Helen steps out, and Ruth can still reach her.

All printed dialogue is public. Receiving the private fragment is silent and acted. The chair and the conversation about Helen's invitation prompt her recollection. Kit receives one remembered instant, then chooses to use it. It is Helen's experience of a moment, not objective evidence settling what the promise meant.

**The quiet trial changes Ruth's choice.** For a sustained interval, Ruth keeps quiet and Helen enjoys her wider life. It works as proposed. Ruth discovers she would be staying partly to wait for a familiar daughter to return. She chooses Sal's invitation to learn a dance instead. Helen initially accepts the departure, then tries to secure visits in the familiar room and form. Ruth stays to challenge the assumption that leaving would mean accepting those conditions. Kit's gathering creates Kit's urgency; it is not a deadline that forces Ruth out.

## Freedom in the draft

The nine movements and their timings are a working route, not a prescribed scene count. The names, private chronology, professions, individual jokes and final image can change where the writing gives a stronger reason. The dialogue is exploratory material to develop or replace, not a voice the writer must imitate line by line.

The early swimming-bag passage is retained from revision 1 and needs integration. The revised middle is continuous, but it does not join directly to that opening: movement two and other linking material remain to be written. The final four movements are outlined, not scripted. No runtime has been established.

The ending is especially open. The current proposal has Ruth leave independently, then Helen choose to accompany Kit in a familiar form. Write it and judge its effect in the complete play. There is no obligation to preserve “just for the arrival,” the empty room, or that particular final action if they make the result too neat.

## Three questions to test through scenes

1. **Does Kit's disclosure stay proportionate?** It should alter trust without making the rest of the play solely about the breach. Kit uses a fragment to advance a case they already wanted to make. Helen's subsequent care need not restore the intimacy she withdrew.
2. **Do these people exceed their descriptions?** Their work, pleasures, bad stories and embarrassments should make them particular and enjoyable. Some details can matter to them without illustrating the play's argument.
3. **Does the ending earn its uncertainty?** Give Ruth's departure its own weight. Let Helen want company, retain mistrust, and be able to refuse Kit. Hear whether the final request invites thought or merely announces a repeated family pattern.

These are questions for the draft and an eventual reading, not requests for another treatment answering every objection.
